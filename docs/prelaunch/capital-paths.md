## Current aggregate evidence

Stanford's 2026 study covers core U.S. and Canadian search funds formed since 1984, with data through December 31, 2025. Stanford's official summary reports a 58% aggregate acquisition rate, roughly 20 months to acquire, a $16 million median purchase price for 2024–2025 acquisitions, and aggregate 33.9% IRR / 4.75x ROI as of the study date. Those historical population figures are not Miles's probability of acquiring, a target-size recommendation, or a return forecast. [Stanford study](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/case-studies/2026-search-fund-study-selected-observations), [official Stanford summary](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/search-funds-keep-offering-proven-path-ownership).

## Shared formulas

```text
search runway
  = months × (monthly personal burn + monthly search overhead)
  + one-time cost + diligence reserve + contingency

total uses
  = purchase consideration + obligations + transaction/financing costs
  + opening working capital + near-term capex + transition/manager cost + contingency

funding gap = total uses - total evidenced sources

manager-adjusted cash flow
  = evidenced operating cash flow - market manager cost
  - maintenance capex - recurring working-capital investment - other normalized obligations

DSCR = manager-adjusted cash flow / required annual principal and interest
```

Unknown inputs remain unknown. No case may be labeled balanced, eligible, indicated, approved, committed, or funded from placeholders.

## Three bounded paths

| Path | Illustrative search runway | Key dependency | Continue gate | Stop / reduce gate |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Traditional search | $500,000 over 20 months | Full-time commitment, investor governance, professionally reviewed capital communications | Protected private runway plus willingness to accept negotiated governance/dilution | Full-time search or governance is unacceptable, reserve is consumed, or professional review rejects the path |
| Self-funded / SBA-style | $116,000 over 12 months | Private liquidity/risk limits plus later target-specific lender review | Personal limits fit target size and a real target later passes lender/professional review | Buyer capital breaches protected liquidity or debt fails downside coverage |
| Independent sponsor | $161,000 over 9 months | Evidenced deal/operating credibility, broken-deal limit, and target-specific capital relationships | Credible thesis and execution fit with tolerable broken-deal exposure | Depends on unearned credibility, capital appearing only after exclusivity, or unacceptable reviewed economics/governance |

All figures are illustrative assumptions from the pre-launch contract, not market benchmarks or Miles's finances.

## Why self-funded-size preparation starts first

It is the most reversible branch for testing a narrow thesis, public-lead screening, private downside gates, and target-level sources/uses without first raising search capital or relying on unverified deal-by-deal capital relationships. It is not a claim that Miles should self-fund an acquisition or qualifies for SBA financing.

SBA states that 7(a) loans may fund qualifying complete or partial ownership changes, have a current maximum loan amount of $5 million, and are applied for through a lender; eligibility includes creditworthiness and reasonable ability to repay. Those program boundaries do not establish borrower, target, structure, loan, guarantee, equity injection, collateral, rate, or approval. [SBA 7(a)](https://www.sba.gov/loans/7a-loans/). At the 2026-08-25 as-of date, SOP 50 10 version 8 (effective 2025-06-01) remains current; version 8.1 is posted but does not become effective until 2026-10-01. [SBA SOP 50 10](https://www.sba.gov/document/sop-50-10-lender-development-company-loan-programs).

Any investor raise or private-fund activity requires qualified securities counsel. The SEC explains that private-fund offerings must fit an exemption, Rule 506(b) generally prohibits general solicitation, Rule 506(c) permits broad solicitation only under conditions including accredited purchasers and reasonable verification, and antifraud rules broadly apply. [SEC Private Funds](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/capital-raising-building-blocks/private-funds), [SEC Rule 506(c)](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings/general-solicitation-rule-506c).
